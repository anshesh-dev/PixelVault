How it actually works.

Watch one letter disappear into three pixels, bit by bit.

Animation: hiding one letter

LSB steganography

Each colour value is 8 bits. Changing only the last bit shifts the colour by 1 out of 256, a difference no eye can see. PixelVault writes your data into those last bits, three per pixel.

AES-256 encryption

With a password, the secret is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before hiding. The key comes from your password via PBKDF2 (200,000 rounds). GCM also detects a wrong password or any tampering.

Self-destruct

Each secret has a random ID. PixelVault records it once a read-once secret is opened, or after too many wrong passwords, and refuses to open it again. The settings are sealed inside the encryption, so they can't be edited.

RS steganalysis

Hiding random bits upsets a natural balance between smooth and rough pixel groups. RS analysis (Fridrich, 2001) measures that imbalance to estimate how much of a photo carries hidden data, with no password needed.

Why PNG, never JPG

PNG is lossless, so every bit survives. JPG and chat apps re-compress photos and scramble the last bits, which erases the secret. Always send the PNG as a document or file.

Pixel X-ray

Bit-plane slicing shows one bit of every colour as black or white. High bits show the picture; bit 0 looks like static. Where data is hidden, that static becomes perfectly uniform noise.
